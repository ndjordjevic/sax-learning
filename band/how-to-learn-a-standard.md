# How to learn a band standard

**Goal for now:** play from the chart (557 Bb edition or iReal Pro), know where the band is, and play correct chord notes. Memorizing is not a goal. This is adapted from `reports/Efficient jazz standard learning.md` plus a 2026-11-18 check of 12 YouTube teacher videos.

1. **Listen** to a short playlist until you can hum the melody: one straight version (a Sinatra or Ella vocal if the tune has words, the original horn version if not) plus 2–3 others. Pick one **companion recording** (ideally a sax player) to copy phrasing from.
2. **Sing** the melody with the recording. Once you know how it sounds, the chart is easy to follow.
3. **Play the melody from the 557** (the band's version) along with the recording.
4. **Name the map:** the key, the form (AABA, ABAC, 16-bar, blues), the ii–V–Is and the turnarounds. Mark section letters on the chart if needed.
5. **Follow the chart over a backing track:** roots (then root + 5th), then guide tones (3rds and 7ths), then arpeggios. Read the chords and keep your place through every section. Work the **hardest section first and most** (often the bridge).
6. **At band:** read the chart, and aim to never lose your place.
7. **Improvise from the chart:** guide tones and chord tones (Playbook), then a phrase copied from the companion recording's solo. Pentatonic phrases (Birch) come back when Birch comes off hold.
8. **Done** when you can follow the chart through the whole form at band and play chord tones on every change. Then move to the next tune.

**Keeping the repertoire:** mark each tune's status in [repertoire.md](repertoire.md). Each session, run through one older tune with the chart (melody + roots). Keep up the Aebersold ear training.

Where to find charts, backing tracks and recordings: [repertoire.md](repertoire.md). Material for one tune: `../tunes/`.

## Following along without playing (before you play at band)

Decided 2026-11-24. Until you play with the band, sit with the chart (the 557, or the Bb tenor sheet) and follow the changes by ear while they play, keeping track in your head of where you are in the form. It's step 6 on its own, without the horn.

**Practice it at home first, in this order:**
1. **A full recording** with the chart. The band's real situation: horns, solos, a different feel from the chart.
2. **A backing track with no melody:** a Playbook track, a Hal Leonard "rhythm section only" track, Tomplay with the sax muted, or Quartet.
3. **iReal Pro:** a chord loop with no melody, in the band's key and tempo. It has no intros, so count them yourself.

**How:**
- Follow the **form first, not every bar:** know where each section starts (A, bridge, last 8) and listen for landmarks you can hear (the turnaround, a key change, the head coming back).
- Keep a finger or pencil on the current bar. If you lose it, wait for the next landmark and rejoin there; don't hunt for the bar.
- **Count choruses** in your head ("solo 2, then the head").
- Start with tunes whose changes you already have (*Autumn Leaves*, *All Blues*, *All of Me*), and stay on one until you can follow a full chorus without getting lost.
- Once that is easy, quietly add the **root, then the guide tones**, in your head.

**At band:** the band may play its own intro, a different number of solo choruses, or its own ending, so treat the chart as a guide. Ask a bandmate for the road map before each tune (who solos, how many choruses, how it ends). If they allow it, record the rehearsal and follow along with the recording at home.

## Hearing the changes (ear training)

All of this uses owned material plus free web tools:
1. **Chord sounds:** Essential Elements Book 1 tenor tracks 31–35 (maj7, 7 and m7 side by side), then track 36 (a blues progression).
2. **Roots:** sing the bass notes over the Playbook *Autumn Leaves* backing (Playbook pp.17–18), then play the roots, then root + 5th (*Let's Play Jazz!* Ch.3).
3. **Guide tones:** play and sing the 3rds and 7ths from the Playbook guide-tone charts.
4. **Quizzes:** **Aebersold *Jazz Ear Training*** (owned). Work Ex.1–20 in order: guess, then check the Bb answer pages (pp.8–13). Ex.25 is ii–V7–I. Extra free drills: teoria.com (Seventh Chord Ear Training → Jazz Progressions, II-V-I) or the iwasdoingallright Online Ear Trainer (jazz mode).
5. **Band tunes:** sing the roots along with a recording before you open the 557, then check.

Full research: `reports/Jazz ear training resources.md`. Possible next buy: [wishlist](../library/wishlist.md).

**Later, when ready:** learn tunes by ear (the Playbook's **LIST** method, pp.15–19), short transcriptions, and Rubank Intermediate as reading material.

## Chord tones: ideas for later

Discussed 2026-11-21. Start simple and add these one at a time when you're ready.
- **Learn chords from the tunes, not all 48 up front.** Each tune teaches its chords in context, and drills on the side fill in the rest.
- **Main chord families:**
  - dominant 7 (1 3 5 ♭7)
  - minor 7 (1 ♭3 5 ♭7)
  - major 7 / 6
  - half-diminished (1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭7)
  - minor 6
  - later: altered dominant, diminished 7, sus4.

  *Autumn Leaves* on tenor has five of them: Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 Fmaj7 · Bm7♭5 E7 Am6.
- **There's less to memorize than 4 × 12 × 4 = 192 notes.** The root is the chord name, and the 5th is nearly always a perfect 5th, so what you really learn is the 12 major 3rds and the 12 ♭7s. The other families are one-note changes from the dominant:
  - maj7: raise the 7th;
  - m7: lower the 3rd;
  - m7♭5: lower the 3rd and the 5th.

  In a dominant, the 3rd and ♭7 are a tritone apart, and each guide tone moves a half-step into the next chord.
- **Drone:** a root drone is enough. Pick it in concert pitch (the tenor chord root minus a whole step):
  - a concert B♭ drone = tenor C (fits Cmaj7, C7 and Cm7);
  - for the minor half of *Autumn Leaves*, a concert G drone = tenor A.

  Hold each chord tone and hear its colour (the 3rd is bright, the ♭7 tense). Sing it, then arpeggiate, then put a simple rhythm on one note.
- **12-key drill:** play the dominant 7 arpeggio 1-3-5-♭7-5-3-1 around the cycle of 4ths (C7 F7 B♭7 E♭7 A♭7 D♭7 F♯7 B7 E7 A7 D7 G7), slowly, saying each chord name. Start with C7 F7 G7 D7 A7 E7. Then:
  1. guide tones only, with half-step voice-leading;
  2. other directions;
  3. minor 7 and major 7;
  4. over an iReal Pro one-chord vamp or a cycle drill.
- **BetterSax Chord Tone Zone** drills (single chord tones in simple rhythms over a blues and *There Will Never Be Another You*) are the same idea on other tunes. See [courses](../library/courses.md).
- **Once chord tones are decent, work on articulation:** the Jazz Lesson Videos *(Bb) Articulation Masterclass* (see the [wishlist](../library/wishlist.md)).
