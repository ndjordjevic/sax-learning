# Study plan: Phases 1–5

The daily routine is in the [README](../README.md). These phases are **frozen** while that daily plan is on trial. Each owned resource is placed by its real difficulty, from deep-reading the PDFs; the full verdict for each book is in [books](../library/books.md) and [courses](../library/courses.md).

**Workload rule:** never run more than 2–3 book streams a day. Rotate the rest across the week.

`🔍` = level checked by a full deep-read · `✅` = done · `🔄` = in progress.

## Phase 1: from-zero jazz on-ramp (weeks 1–10). 🔄 In progress
Nothing here assumes any jazz skill.
- Run Reading Jazz and EE Ex.1–30 together: the same skill from two angles.
- Birch can start alongside: Ch.1–3 need no swing reading, and Ch.4 teaches swung vs straight 8ths itself. **Birch is on hold** for now (since 2026-11-21).
- Watch the Harmonic Foundation theory videos **before EE Ex.31**, because Ex.37–39 assume you know what a 3rd and a 7th are.
- EE Ex.31–39 come last.

| # | Resource | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🔍 **Reading Jazz** (Rizzo) D1–6: ✅ D1–4 · 🔄 on Duet 5 | Quarter notes → 8ths → dotted 8th–16th → anticipations. D6 is a review. |
| 2 | 🔍 **BetterSax Core Essentials**: ✅ done | Tone and technique. Its warm-ups stay in the daily fundamentals. |
| 3 | 🔍 **Essential Elements** (Steinel) Ex.1–39 | Ex.1–30: jazz articulation, swing 8ths, syncopation; Ex.27 *London Bridge* is the first "make up your own". ⭐ Ex.31–39: maj7/dom7/min7 and chord symbols → the 12-bar blues → soloing on roots/3rds/7ths, ending in 2-bar solos. Stop at Ex.39. |
| 4 | 🔍 **Beginner Jazz Soloing** (Birch) Ch.1–8 (⏸ on hold) | The item that actually teaches soloing. Ch.1–6 are Birch's six steps; Ch.7 is soloing over 5 backing tracks (from ♩=90); Ch.8 is articulation. **Exit test:** a coherent chorus over the Ch.7 backings at 90 bpm. Ch.9 is optional; Ch.10 belongs in late Phase 2. |
| 5 | 🔍 **BetterSax Harmonic Foundation**, theory videos 01–05 only | Intervals, major scale, chords, functions: exactly what EE Ex.31–39 assume. The 12-key drills come in Phase 3+. |

## Phase 2: swing reading → first tunes (months 2–6)
Rotate 2–3 streams a day.

| # | Resource | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 🔍 **Reading Jazz** D7–19 | **D7 is the real jump**: it starts the syncopated-quarter-note family that runs to D19. 1–2 duets a week. |
| 6b | 🔍 **Essential Elements** Ex.40–82 | Dominant-scale steps over the blues → *St. Louis Blues* and improvising on the melody → blues scale, riffs, bends, Q&A. Skim 60–64 (Birch already taught the blues scale). |
| 6c | 🔍 **Easy Jazz Conception** (Snidero) Et.1–3 | The first written etudes. Start after EE Ex.1–39. Method: copy fragments of the demo by ear → play with the soloist → play with the rhythm section only. |
| 6d | 🔍 **O'Neill *Jazz Method*** Ch.10–12, 16–20, 22–23 (supplement) | Skip Ch.1–9 and 13–15 (they repeat Rubank, RJ, EE and Birch). Keep: Ch.11 ear training; Ch.16–17 off-beat phrases; Ch.18 modes (Dorian solo on *Euphrates*, no backing because the CD is missing); Ch.19–20 range and cut time; Ch.22 *Doxy*, *St Thomas*, *Blue Samba*; Ch.23. Work them in order. |
| 6e | 🔍 **Let's Play Jazz!** (Jacobsen) Ch.1–2, and read Ch.8 | Ch.1–2: reading and learning the *Falling Leaves* head. Ch.8 (learning a new standard from the chart) is worth reading now. |
| 6f | 🔍 **Beginner Jazz Soloing** Ch.10 (finishes the book) | One scale over an E♭ ii–V–I vamp: a gentle first contact with ii–V–I. |
| ref | 🔍 **Terefenko** Ch.1–2 | Dip in only for concepts you run into. |

## Phase 3: blues + harmony (months 6–10)
| # | Resource | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 🔍 **Let's Play Jazz!** Ch.3–4 | Root-5th, arpeggios and scales of every *Falling Leaves* chord, including ø7, 7♭9/♯9 and the altered scale, marked 120–180 bpm: work them under tempo. Ch.4 expects instant recall. |
| 8 | 🔍 **BetterSax Blues Foundation** (fast review) **+ Blues Language** | Blues Foundation is mostly a review of Birch + EE by now: focus on the 3 blues heads, "Advanced Ear Training Blues" and "Transcribe This?!". Blues Language (licks in 12 keys, **by ear**) is the ear-training track for this phase. |
| 9 | 🔍 **BetterSax Harmonic Foundation**, drills | Modes, minor scales, circle of 4ths/5ths in 12 keys. "Years of practice": keep chipping away through Phase 4. |
| 10 | 🔍 **Reading Jazz** D20–30 (finishes the book) | Even 8ths, triplets, kickbeats, Charleston, punctuation, quarter-note triplets, and D30 *Triplication* (a 12-bar blues). |
| 10a | 🔍 **Easy Jazz Conception** Et.4–7 | Where the book gets harder: dense 8ths, straight-8th rock, modal (♩=165), shuffle. Pairs well with the BetterSax blues modules. |
| 10b | 🔍 **Essential Elements** Ex.101–120 | Even 8ths vs swing, Latin/rock, Dorian. In the book these come after Ex.83–100; the plan does them first on purpose. |
| 10c | 🔍 **O'Neill** Ch.21, 24 | Scales in all keys and the cycle of 4ths (Ch.21); harmony, ii–V–I and chord-tone voice-leading on *Fall '90* (Ch.24). |
| listen | 🔍 **The Essence of the Blues**: listening tracks only | Absorb Eric Alexander's phrasing. **Don't play the etudes yet.** |
| ref | 🔍 **Terefenko** Ch.6 (ii–V–I), 7 (modes), 9 (blues) | The theory behind what BetterSax and Essence teach by ear. |

## Phase 4: intermediate, changes-based playing (months 10–18)
| # | Resource | Why here |
|---|---|---|

| 13 | 🔍 **Rhythm Changes Soloing** (Birch) Ch.1–7 | Ch.1–4 chord-tone soloing on the A section; Ch.5–6 substitutions; Ch.7 the bridge (its "Eternal Triangle" example waits for Phase 5). Ch.1–4 are already used for step 5 of the daily plan. |
| 14 | First intermediate books from the [wishlist](../library/wishlist.md) | *Jazz Phrasing 3*, *Effective Etudes Vol.1*, *Reading Key Jazz Rhythms*, etc. |
| ref | 🔍 **Terefenko** Ch.10 (improvisation) | The theory side of the above. |
