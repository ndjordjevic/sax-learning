# Owned books (by level)

Every level here comes from a full deep-read of the PDF (`🔍`), not from publisher blurbs. Where the books sit in the plan: [README](../README.md) (Phase 0) and [study plan](../plan/study-plan.md). Bookmarked books that aren't owned are in the [wishlist](wishlist.md). The library is on pCloud: `~/pCloudDrive/iCloud-Migration/MyBookLive/Sax/`.

**Type:** Method = a full course · Etudes = written solos to absorb · Phrasing = vocabulary studies · Theory = reference · Reading = rhythm-reading drills.

## Beginner
| Book | Type | Sax | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔍 **Beginner Jazz Soloing for Sax & Clarinet** (Birch) | Method | Alto/Tenor (per-instrument audio) | A 6-step, ear-first guide built on **one scale, G minor pentatonic** (+ the blues scale; chords only in Ch.10). From zero. 153 audio examples. Ch.1–6: from 1 note to 5, minor pentatonic + blues scale, scale patterns (tracks from 60 bpm), rhythmic phrasing, melodies, Q&A. Ch.7: 5 backings (bossa, swing, pop, minor and major blues) at 90/120/150 bpm (blues up to 180). Ch.8: articulation (ghost notes, upbeat accents, vibrato, growl, glissando). Ch.9: composition (optional). Ch.10: G minor pentatonic over an E♭ ii–V–I, then C minor pentatonic. It isn't tied to any tune: use its own backing tracks. |
| 🔍 **Essential Elements for Jazz Ensemble** (Steinel) | Method | **Tenor** paper book (HL 00841348) + tenor PDF scan (33 pp, image-only) + tenor audio (tracks 1–121) | **Spans Phases 1–4**: 121 exercises, from "what is a swing 8th" to bebop enclosure. Strong on soloing on roots/3rds/7ths, **paraphrase improv** (decorate the melody, don't run a scale), Doo/Bah/Dit/Dot articulation, bebop vocabulary, Mixolydian and Dorian. Never teaches the pentatonic scale. Tracks 31–36 are the owned chord-sound listening audio. Ex.27, 39 and 44 trade bars with the band tracks. Phase map below. pCloud `Essential Elements for Jazz Ensemble - Mike Steinel (Hal Leonard, 2000)/` (+ `Audio - Tenor/`; the alto PDF is there too). |

### Essential Elements: phase map
Ex.31 starts chord theory, a real step up. The Phase 1/2 boundary is Ex.40, because 31–39 (soloing on roots/3rds/7ths) is worth pulling into late Phase 1. The book doesn't use the term "guide tones"; O'Neill Ch.24 covers the same idea. **Book order:** 83–100 (pp.24–27) come *before* 101–120 (pp.28–31); the plan reverses them on purpose.

| Ex. | Content | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| **1–24** | Attacks/releases, accenting 2 & 4, Doo/Bah/Dit/Dot, swing 8ths, syncopation, *Ja-Da*, *When the Saints* | 1 |
| **25–30** | "Jazzin' up" melodies; **Ex.27 = first improv**; *Swingin' With Jack* | 1 |
| **31–39** | maj7/dom7/min7 + chord symbols (31–35), **12-bar blues progression** (36), **soloing on roots/3rds/7ths** → 2-bar solos (37–39) | late 1 |
| **40–45** | Dominant scale, dominant-scale workouts over the blues (41–43), *Our First Blues* | 2 |
| **46–59** | *St. Louis Blues*: 2nd key, **improvising on the melody**, demo solo | 2 |
| **60–82** | Blues scale (skim 60–64 after Birch), riffs & licks, major blues scale, bends & scoops, **Q&A** | 2 |
| **101–120** | Even 8ths vs swing, Latin/rock, *Salsa Caliente*, **Dorian** from the min7 chord | 3 |
| **83–100** | **Bebop**: Mixolydian, chromatic notes, **enclosure**, bebop lick & scale | 4 |
| **121** + pp.32–33 | 7 ornaments, chord-scale table, scales in 8 keys | reference |

## Easy → Intermediate
| Book | Type | Sax | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔍 **The Jazz Standards Playbook Vol. 1** (Brent Vaartstra, Learn Jazz Standards, 2018) | Method | Bb + Eb PDFs; one shared audio set at concert pitch | 193 pp, **easy → intermediate, theory-led**. 10 "Master Standards": *Autumn Leaves, Blue Bossa, All of Me, It Could Happen to You, Sweet Georgia Brown, So What, On Green Dolphin Street, Have You Met Miss Jones, All the Things You Are, Stella by Starlight*; 9 of the 10 are in the 557 (not *So What*). Opens with the **LIST** ear method (Listen, Internalize, Sing, Transfer; pp.15–19, including singing the roots). Each study has suggested recordings, a **chord map**, a **guide-tone chart**, a Roman-numeral analysis and one improv lesson. The lessons go: guide tones → using the melody → chord tones → composing melodies → cycling dominants → modes → motifs → diminished licks → composed solo → **note maps** (*Stella*). Exercises start in half/quarter notes and grow to 8th-note composed solos. **No melodies printed** (copyright): learn the heads by ear or from the 557. Common concert keys (*Autumn Leaves* concert G minor = tenor A minor, the same as the band). Audio: 3 backing mixes per tune + demos of every exercise. |
| 🔍 **Let's Play Jazz!** (Martin Jacobsen, Advance Music 2025) | Method | Bb/Eb in one book; one shared audio set | **8 chapters, all on one tune**, *Falling Leaves* (Autumn Leaves changes, concert G minor). Ch.1–2: reading and the head. Ch.3–4: chord/scale/arpeggio drills including ø7, 7♭9/♯9 and the altered scale at 120–180 bpm. Ch.5–6: bebop, superimposition, tritone subs. Ch.7: other keys. Ch.8: how to learn a new standard (from the chart and analysis, not by ear). Audio: 6 tracks (head, play-alongs, easy chorus ♩=120, advanced choruses). Schott rates it Grade 3, but from Ch.3 on it's changes-based and steep: slow down there, or let the Playbook lead. Same tune and key as Playbook Study #1. |
| 🔍 **Reading Jazz** (Rizzo) | Reading | Tenor **and** alto editions + CD | 30 duets, rhythm-reading from quarter notes up to a 12-bar blues. No improv and no chords printed (work them out per duet; *First Time* on tenor: Em · A7 · B7 · C). The alto edition is the same duets at the same concert pitch with the same CD, so one audio set serves both horns. All 30 duets are channel-split + sax-removed (see `audio-processing-methodology.md`); archive in pCloud `…/Reading Jazz - Alto and Tenor Sax - Jacques Rizzo (Alfred, 1996)/Audio/`. |
| 🔍 **Easy Jazz Conception** (Snidero) | Etudes | Tenor + alto PDFs; **tenor MP3 pack** | **Easy–intermediate, not beginner** ("easy" only next to Snidero's *Jazz Conception*). 15 two-page etudes: swing, blues, bossa, rock, waltz; ♩=108→165; written range low C♯ (subtone) to palm E♭, no altissimo. Et.1–10 are fully marked; 11–15 are "real charts" with few markings. Jazz harmony (alt, ♭9/♯11) from Et.2. Et.1–3: *Basie's Blues* (C blues), *Morning Calm* (D minor), *So Long Birdie* (G). Audio (pCloud `…/Easy Jazz Conception - Jim Snidero (Advance Music, 1999)/Audio - Tenor/`): 1–15 demos (Eric Alexander), 16–17 tuning notes, 18–32 play-alongs. Both editions use the same concert keys and track numbers. |
| 🔍 **The Jazz Method for Tenor Saxophone** (O'Neill, Schott ED 12420, 1992) | Method | Tenor; **CD missing** | 99 pp, **beginner → low intermediate**. 26 chapters, from assembling the horn up to 16th notes, low B♭–high F♯, ii–V–I, and Monk/Rollins/Silver/Parker heads. Improv stays scale-over-vamp (pentatonic, blues, Dorian). Unique: an **ear-training chapter** (Ch.11), real tunes, rich listening lists. Band tunes in it: *Doxy* (Ch.22), *My Little Suede Shoes* (Parker coda). The book says "don't depart from the order". The CD had the sax panned to one channel, so a copy could be split like Reading Jazz. The revised edition (wishlist) has online audio. |
| 🔍 **Jazz Phrasing for Saxophone 1** (Fishman) | Phrasing | Alto/Tenor, one written part; **no audio** | **Beginner → intermediate.** 10 written tunes: easy through #3, clearly intermediate from #4 (*Belden*: 56-bar bossa, ♭9 chords). Material to absorb, not an improv method. Complete app-edition PDF. |
| 🔍 **Jazz Phrasing for Saxophone 2** (Fishman) | Phrasing | Alto/Tenor; **no audio** | The cover says Beginning-Intermediate, but it's a step harder than Vol. 1: ♩=112–196 and more styles. 10 tunes: 7 swing (*Castlewood Terrace*, *Garfield Blvd*, *Hyde Park Blvd*, *Pulaski Road* blues, *Lexington Ave*, *Edgewater Ave* at 196), plus *Dayton Street* (samba), *Orchard Street* (bossa), *Fargo Avenue* (ballad, ♩=86), *School Street* (shuffle). |
| 🔍 **The Essence of the Blues** (Snidero) | Etudes | Tenor (tenor-only audio) | **Intermediate → advanced**, and it assumes fluent swing-8th reading. 10 style etudes; the difficulty zig-zags by era. Listen now; easier etudes in Phase 4, the back half in Phase 5. |

## Horn and key
Mainly tenor, so use the Bb/tenor edition when there's a choice; alto material is still usable.
- **Essential Elements:** use the tenor books (Books 1 and 2) with the tenor audio. The alto PDF reads G7 for a B♭-concert blues, so on tenor it sounds a whole tone off the tracks.
- **Owned in both editions:** the Playbook (one shared concert-pitch audio set) and *Easy Jazz Conception* (use the tenor book; the alto CD soloist is Snidero, and its cover misprints "21 etudes").
- **One book for both horns:** *Let's Play Jazz!*, Birch (both books), Fishman *Jazz Phrasing*, BetterSax.
- **Tenor only:** the Snidero *Essence* books, O'Neill, Dennis Taylor, the AB Real Book and Real Book Vol. 2 (Bb editions owned).
