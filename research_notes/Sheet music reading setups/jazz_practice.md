# What jazz musicians (esp. horn players) actually use to read charts, and how they organize fake books digitally (2020–2026)

Context for the report writer: the user is a tenor sax player in a weekly amateur band that reads from the *557 Jazz Standards* fake book (C edition), on an iPad with MobileSheets, sometimes mirrored to a TV.

Source-quality warning: Reddit threads did not come up in any search, and jazzguitar.be (the biggest jazz-forum source) returned HTTP 403 when fetched. The jazzguitar.be findings below therefore come from search-result snippets, not full reads. Many Sax on the Web threads on transposition are old (2007–2014), and I mark them as such. Some of the 2026 "app comparison" sites are affiliate or review sites. Treat everything from forums as opinion or anecdote.

## 1. Paper vs tablet vs phone at gigs, jams and rehearsals

### Takeaway
On jazz forums, tablets (mostly iPad) are now the normal tool among players who read at gigs. The objections are practical (keep a backup, charge it, screen glow on stage) and cultural: jam-session etiquette still favours knowing tunes by ear, with a phone running iReal Pro accepted as a discreet fallback for a tune you don't know. I found no reliable statistic on what share of players use tablets.

### Cited Findings
- Opinion: a jazz-guitar forum poster who gigs regularly says the iPad "just makes more sense" for jazz, but would still use paper for long classical sessions. — [jazzguitar.be: anyone still using sheet music](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/everything-else/97309-anyone-still-using-sheet-music.html) (snippet only)
- Reliability (anecdote): one poster has used an iPad with iReal Pro for 3+ years with no problems, but says a gig-critical iPad needs a backup "like a guitar and amp". Another says the battery lasts a 3-hour gig. — [jazzguitar.be: reliability of iPads at gigs](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/guitar-amps-gizmos/61856-reliability-ipads-gig.html) (snippet only)
- Battery (anecdote): a Gearspace user has "never had the battery run out during a gig". Another says battery life is excellent but charging is slow, so they bring a charger. On lighting, a UK poster says an iPad on a mic stand is "very discreet and more importantly is always well lit", which solves the old problem of reading paper charts in a dark room. — [Gearspace: Gigging musicians, what are you using for charts?](https://gearspace.com/threads/gigging-musicians-what-are-you-using-for-charts-ipad-android-etc.865863/)
- Stage look (opinion): a TalkBass thread objects to the "sickly glow of iPad light on your face" on stage. A suggested fix is a dark mode (white notes on a black background). — [TalkBass: tablets for charts](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/tablets-for-charts-time-for-an-update.1076663/)
- Adoption (anecdote): most musicians that one poster knows who use iPads at gigs load PDF fake books into a reader app. — [Gearspace thread](https://gearspace.com/threads/gigging-musicians-what-are-you-using-for-charts-ipad-android-etc.865863/)
- Jam etiquette: the Elon University jazz-studies etiquette pages say to memorize tunes rather than read sheet music, and to avoid reading from a phone during a performance (there are separate pages for horn players and singers). — [Elon: Jam Session Etiquette for Horn Players](https://www.elon.edu/u/academics/arts-and-sciences/jazz-studies/?p=1713); [Elon: for Singers](https://www.elon.edu/u/academics/arts-and-sciences/jazz-studies/?p=1703) (the page refused connection on fetch; content from snippets)
- Softer view: No Treble's etiquette piece says that when an unfamiliar tune is called, a player can stay composed and check the changes on their phone. — [No Treble: Jam Session Etiquette](https://www.notreble.com/buzz/2016/06/01/jam-session-etiquette/) (2016)
- Learn Jazz Standards suggests bringing "an iPad with iReal Pro or a real book" to a jam in case an unfamiliar tune comes up, and building a list of at least ten tunes you know. — [LearnJazzStandards: Jazz Jam Sessions](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/learning-jazz/jazz-advice/jazz-jam-sessions-important/)
- Forum split on reading at jams (opinions): one poster saw an organist reading iReal on his phone while nobody else used charts. Another says letting people read charts "is not an unreasonable thing to expect at a jam". A third describes a host scolding a player for reading and calls that harsh. Another says that when people read, "a lot of the awareness gets switched off". — [jazzguitar.be: who uses iReal Pro at jams/performance](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/recording-music-software/94406-who-uses-ireal-pro-jams-performance.html) (snippet only)
- Opinion: reading can hurt interaction, because players "with their faces in books" are harder to coordinate with. A counter-view says charts don't limit improvisation at all. — [jazzguitar.be: using charts vs memorizing standards](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/improvisation/64003-using-charts-vs-memorizing-jazz-standards.html) (snippet only)
- A bandleader calls an iPad with iReal essential for jams where audience members pick tunes and singers choose keys. — search result on the [iReal Pro forums](https://forums.irealpro.com/goto/post?id=3630) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- For a weekly amateur band that reads from a shared fake book, a tablet is the norm, not a social problem. The "learn it by ear" norm matters mostly at open jam sessions with stronger players. This fits the user's goal of reading the 557 chart and keeping place (see repo memory).
- The practical risk list repeated across forums is: battery and charger, a backup (a phone with the same PDFs or iReal Pro), a stand or clamp at reading height, and dark mode or low brightness on dim stages.

### Gaps
- No survey or percentage of jazz players using tablets vs paper, 2020–2026.
- No Reddit (r/Jazz, r/saxophone) threads surfaced. Sax on the Web iPad-at-gigs threads did not surface either, apart from an archived "Real Book apps for iPad" thread.
- No sourced discussion of outdoor glare or sunlight readability for jazz gigs.

## 2. Horn players: the C-book vs Bb transposition problem on a tablet

### Takeaway
A PDF reader cannot transpose a scanned PDF. Horn players handle this in one of four ways: buy or load the Bb edition of the same book (Hal Leonard Real Books come in C, Bb, Eb and bass clef, and apps like iGigBook can group the editions and switch between them); transpose at sight from the C book (read up a major 2nd and add 2 sharps / remove 2 flats, the usual advice for a band that reads from a C book); use iReal Pro with global Bb display for the chords; or re-enter the tune as MusicXML/notation and transpose it in software. Forum opinion is split on whether to rely on Bb books or learn to read concert.

### Cited Findings
- 2007 Sax on the Web poll (28 votes): 60.7% preferred concert fake books and 39.3% transposed books. Arguments given: owning C, Bb and Eb copies of every book is costly; buying in C lets you photocopy tunes for the rhythm section. — [SOTW: Transposed Real Book or concert?](https://www.saxontheweb.net/threads/transposed-real-book-or-concert.51480/page-2) (old)
- Opinion in favour of the C book: it prepares you to read a tune from a piano or bass player's book at a gig and to rehearse in concert pitch. Opinion in favour of the Bb book: you learn changes and melodies faster without transposing. — [SOTW: Bb fake book and transposition](https://www.saxontheweb.net/threads/bb-fake-book-and-transposition.194039/); [SOTW: Transposing from fake book](https://www.saxontheweb.net/threads/transposing-from-fake-book.87757/); [SOTW: Should I get a Real Book?](https://www.saxontheweb.net/threads/should-i-get-a-real-book.150301/) (mostly c. 2010–2014)
- Tenor transposition shortcut: read up a major 2nd, like clarinet, soprano and trumpet, remembering that tenor sounds an octave lower. — [Best Saxophone Website Ever: guide to transposition](https://bestsaxophonewebsiteever.com/the-saxophonists-guide-to-the-dreaded-art-of-transposition/); [Cafe Saxophone](https://cafesaxophone.com/threads/sheet-music-question.8509/)
- Software route (old forum tip): enter the chords in concert key, then add a Bb sax instrument to see a transposed part (Band-in-a-Box). — [PG Music forum: transpose for tenor sax using fake book and BIAB](https://www.pgmusic.com/forums/ubbthreads.php?ubb=printthread&Board=1&main=9299&type=thread)
- iGigBook (vendor claim): switches between the Bb, C, Eb and bass-clef PDFs of the same book, with indexes for 70–90+ fake books. You supply the PDFs yourself. — [iGigBook App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id396323113). A SOTW user praised grouping the editions to switch mid-song; another found the setup tedious. — [SOTW archive: Real Book apps for iPad](https://forum.saxontheweb.net/archive/index.php/t-208971.html)
- Index mismatch pitfall: one user's index for the New Real Book digital edition matched the print edition, which has different songs on different pages. — [iGigBook App Store listing reviews](https://apps.apple.com/app/id396323113)
- iReal Pro: the global "Transposing instrument → Bb" setting changes how chords are displayed in every chart, while playback stays in concert pitch. Per-song transpose changes the chart's key and its playback. Charts are stored and edited only in concert key. A known gap: the scale/chord helper stays in concert pitch, and users work around it by switching the display font. — [iReal Pro forum: Transpose for tenor sax](https://forums.irealpro.com/threads/transpose-for-tenor-sax.9355); [iReal Pro forum: scales/chords for transposed instruments](https://forums.irealpro.com/threads/scales-chords-for-transposed-instruments.19888/latest); [iReal Pro forum: writing charts for transposed instruments](https://forums.irealpro.com/threads/writing-new-charts-for-transposed-instruments.17903/latest)
- Hal Leonard's Real Book Vol. I (6th ed.) is published in C, Bb, Eb and bass clef editions. — [Hal Leonard Real Book Vol I Bb](https://www.halleonard.com/product/240224/the-real-book-volume-i-sixth-edition?searchcategory=8); [Hal Leonard Real Books feature](https://www.halleonard.com/feature/490/real-books)
- Free option: SaxTeacher UK offers lead-sheet PDFs in concert, Bb and Eb. — [saxteacheruk.com online real book](https://saxteacheruk.com/transcriptions/real-book-free-lead-sheet-pdf-downloads.html)
- *557 Jazz Standards* Bb edition: no publisher-listed Bb edition surfaced. Pages offering a "full 557 jazz standards in Bb" download look like spam or pirate sites and should be avoided. — e.g. [sites.google.com "full-557-jazz-standards-in-bb"](https://sites.google.com/view/full-557-jazz-standards-in-bb) (cited only as a warning)

### Inferences
- When the whole band reads a C book (the 557), the tenor player has two choices. One is to read the C chart and transpose at sight: same page, same layout, same rehearsal letters as everyone else. The other is to make Bb versions tune by tune (the user already does this: "`<Tune> - Bb Tenor Sax (from 557 Jazz Standards).pdf`" files and MuseScore lead sheets). A Bb version from a different book can change the layout, bar numbers and even the changes, which makes it harder to keep your place when the leader says "from the bridge". Laying out the Bb copies to match the 557 page avoids this.
- In MobileSheets, a common pattern is to keep the 557 C page and the Bb version as two files for the same song (or use a song-versions feature once one exists) so you can flip between them.

### Gaps
- No 2020–2026 source specifically on reading lead sheets on a tablet while standing (stand height, clamp, iPad size). Only general mic-stand mentions.
- I did not verify current MobileSheets or forScore transposition features (the apps researcher covers this).

## 3. How amateur bands share charts

### Takeaway
In practice, each player keeps their own library on their own device. The band shares a setlist of titles, not the PDFs. MobileSheets does this with an exported `.mss` setlist that matches each member's own files by title or Song ID. Newzik and BandHelper offer true shared libraries but need subscriptions. Showing one screen to the whole room by mirroring to a TV over HDMI or AirPlay is a recognised rehearsal technique.

### Cited Findings
- MobileSheets "Configuration for bands" thread (Aug 2023–Feb 2025): Zubersoft's recommended method is Share → Export song list to a `.mss` file, sent to each member. On import it rebuilds the setlist from that member's own PDFs, matched by exact title or by the Song ID field, which overrides title matching. Library sync can't be used when members need different versions. Workarounds: per-device folders holding same-named files with different content, or "Connect Tablets", which matches by title. `.mss` is XML and can be generated outside the app. For members without the app, "Generate Song List" with file paths lets the leader combine PDFs. A jazz-band user asked for per-instrument parts (chords for rhythm section, lyrics for vocals, brass parts, drum notation) on one setlist. Zubersoft said in Aug 2024 that song versions with "roles" were planned for v3.11.0, but the thread does not confirm a release. — [MobileSheets forum: Configuration for bands](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/thread-10362.html)
- Newzik: shared Projects carry scores and annotations between users. Distributing parts is a paid feature. Leader/follower page turns were removed in v6.0 (April 2021). — [sheetmusicapps.com Newzik review 2026](https://www.sheetmusicapps.com/reviews/newzik/) (review site)
- BandHelper: one subscription syncs songs, setlists, chord charts, PDFs and backing tracks to every member, with admin roles and per-player parts. — [sheetmusicapps.com](https://www.sheetmusicapps.com/) (review site)
- One screen for the whole band: Newzik documents HDMI output, either mirroring or showing only the active part. — [Newzik support: using an external screen](https://support.newzik.com/en/support/solutions/articles/77000467387-using-an-external-screen). Planning Center Music Stand supports a TV "so everyone can see the same screen". — [Planning Center: connect to an external display](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/connect-to-an-external-display.html). An Apple TV with AirPlay mirroring is the standard wireless route; wired HDMI is more dependable. — [MTNA iPad handout](https://www.mtna.org/downloads/Conference/Handouts/2013/Tuesday/InspiredUsesOfTheiPadsAndMobileDevices.pdf); [Digital Trends: connect iPad to TV](https://www.digitaltrends.com/home-theater/how-to-connect-ipad-to-tv/); [Sweetwater: iPad display methods](https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/ipad-display-methods/)
- Oberlin: everyone pulling up the score on their own iPad (forScore and a Bluetooth pedal) sped up rehearsal. — [Oberlin: The Great Screen Test](https://www.oberlin.edu/index%2Ephp/news/great-screen-test)

### Inferences
- With a projected C chart, a tenor player still has to transpose (or have a Bb copy on their own device). A TV works well for keeping everyone on the same tune and form, but in a mixed C/Bb/Eb band it can't replace each player's own part.
- For a weekly amateur band on a single fake book, the low-cost workflow matches what the forums describe: everyone owns the same book, the leader sends a list of tune titles and page numbers, and each person's app jumps to the page from its index. The page number in the 557 is the shared key.

### Gaps
- No 2020–2026 forum source on how small amateur jazz combos in particular share charts (WhatsApp lists, Google Drive, printed copies). Most sources are big-band, church or brass-band contexts.
- No first-hand reports on TV-projected lead sheets for jazz combos specifically.

## 4. Indexing fake books, and legal digital sources

### Takeaway
The standard workflow is: one full-book PDF, plus a community CSV index (title → page), imported into forScore or MobileSheets. The usual work is fixing the page offset between printed page numbers and PDF page numbers, and checking that the index matches your exact edition. Legal digital fake books are limited. Hal Leonard's digital Real Books stream from its MyLibrary site (no PDF for forScore or MobileSheets). Sher Music sells at least one downloadable PDF (*The Digital Standards Songbook*).

### Cited Findings
- Index files: the GitHub repo ndp/musicindex holds Real Book indexes for forScore. aspiers/book-indices is another collection. — [github.com/ndp/musicindex](https://github.com/ndp/musicindex); [github.com/aspiers/book-indices](https://github.com/aspiers/book-indices)
- forScore: Bookmarks → Indexes → choose a CSV, map its fields, and set an offset (usually subtracted from the printed page). — [Robby Burns: Indexing Real Books in forScore](https://robbyburns.com/blog/uac5awql1zh7vdrabhbdhmgxhmjl6h)
- MobileSheets: the CSV and the PDF must share a base filename and folder, then Import → Import CSV. Some users needed semicolon delimiters. Offsets of a few pages are a common problem. Index quality varies, and requests for some books (Old and New Real Book volumes) went unanswered. — [MobileSheets forum tid=7933](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/showthread.php?tid=7933); [tid=4542](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/showthread.php?tid=4542); [thread-8473](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/thread-8473.html); [pid=36179 (Jan 2022)](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/showthread.php?pid=36179)
- Calypso Score (vendor claim): ships pre-built indexes for dozens of jazz and pop fake books. — [Calypso Score App Store](https://apps.apple.com/fr/app/calypso-score-sheet-music/id480475970?l=en-GB)
- Hal Leonard digital books are cloud-based and stream with an internet connection via an access code and MyLibrary. Hal Leonard calls its Real Books "the first legitimate and legal editions" (2004). — [Hal Leonard Real Book product page](https://halleonard.com/product/240221); [Real Book (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Real_Book)
- Sher Music *The Digital Standards Songbook*: a downloadable PDF of 176 standards, sold only as a complete book. — [Sher Music: Digital Standards Songbook](https://www.shermusic.com/products/the-digital-standards-songbook)
- SOTW members said licensing (the songs are owned by third parties) is why commercial apps rarely ship the full Real Book. Apps instead index books you already own. — [SOTW archive: Real Book apps for iPad](https://forum.saxontheweb.net/archive/index.php/t-208971.html)

### Inferences
- For the 557 in MobileSheets, the efficient path is to build one CSV index for the C edition (title, page, with the right offset), named the same as the PDF. Then every band tune is a song with a correct page link, and Bb copies can be added as a second file or version of the same song.
- A streaming-only Hal Leonard purchase doesn't fit a MobileSheets or offline-gig workflow.

### Gaps
- I did not find a public CSV index for *557 Jazz Standards*.
- Musicnotes and other per-tune legal PDF sources were not researched in depth (out of time; the apps researcher may cover them).

## 5. Tips and pitfalls from forums (consolidated)

### Takeaway
The recurring advice: carry a backup device, charge it and bring the charger, use the right index for the exact edition, fix page offsets, use dark mode on dim stages, keep the phone discreet at jams, and don't let reading replace listening.

### Cited Findings
- Backup device and charger. — [jazzguitar.be reliability thread](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/guitar-amps-gizmos/61856-reliability-ipads-gig.html); [Gearspace](https://gearspace.com/threads/gigging-musicians-what-are-you-using-for-charts-ipad-android-etc.865863/)
- Check that the index matches the edition (print vs digital New Real Book). — [iGigBook App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/app/id396323113)
- Offsets and delimiters in CSV imports. — [MobileSheets forum](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/showthread.php?tid=7933)
- Dark background to cut glow on stage. — [TalkBass](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/tablets-for-charts-time-for-an-update.1076663/)
- Reading reduces awareness, so look up from the chart (opinion). — [jazzguitar.be](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/recording-music-software/94406-who-uses-ireal-pro-jams-performance.html)
- Use iReal Pro to prepare, but prioritise playing with real musicians. — [LearnJazzStandards: When to use iReal Pro](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/video/when-to-use-ireal-pro-to-learn-jazz/); [5 ways iReal Pro will help (and 4 it won't)](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/?p=184687)
- Avoid pirate "557 in Bb" download pages. — see section 2.

### Inferences
- The user's current setup (iPad + MobileSheets + the 557 C edition + their own Bb copies + occasional TV mirroring) matches what forum players describe as mainstream. The main improvements suggested by the sources are a CSV index for the 557, a backup device, and setlists matched by page or title.

### Gaps
- No primary-source YouTube summaries reviewed.
- Nothing found from 2024–2026 specifically on foot pedals and page turns for horn players on one-page lead sheets. Most jazz lead sheets are one page, so this may matter little.
