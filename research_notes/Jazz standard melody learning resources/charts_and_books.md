# Sheet-music and chart sources for learning jazz-standard melodies (Bb / tenor focus)

Scope note: research done 2026-11-22, about 22 tool calls. Several retailer and forum pages were reachable only through search snippets. Where a claim rests on a snippet rather than a fetched page, it says so. Things not covered are listed under Gaps.

## Q1. Which fake books have the most accurate melodies? (Sher, Hal Leonard 6th ed., 557, Standards Real Book, Colorado Cookbook, iReal) Is there a Bb 557?

### Takeaway
The opinion that keeps coming up is that Sher Music's New Real Book series is the most accurate. Its drawback is that it often prints full arrangements that take up a lot of space. The Hal Leonard 6th-edition Real Book is the legal clean-up of the bootleg Real Book. It fixed many old errors but introduced some new ones, and it is the book most people use at jams. I found no publisher, review, or legitimate Bb edition for the "557 Jazz Standards." The only "557 in Bb" pages online look like spam or piracy.

### Cited Findings
- Sher Music's New Real Books are judged likely the most accurate, but "many contain tunes with full arrangements" that make them hard for an average player to read. — [Keller, The Joy of Fakebooks (Harvey Mudd)](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~keller/jazz/TheJoyOfFakebooks.htm)
- The Real Book 6th edition "was said to have corrected all the mistakes in the Fifth Edition. However, some new ones were introduced." The same author recommends the Hal Leonard Real Book series for reading on the gig. — [Keller, The Joy of Fakebooks](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~keller/jazz/TheJoyOfFakebooks.htm)
- Forum posters call the Sher New Real Book series "quite accurate." One says there are "a few questionable keys in the Hal Leonard series, but overall no glaring errors." Another says that at a jam "it'll be the Hal Leonard real book on everyone's tablet." (forum, anecdotal; page returned 403 so this comes from the search snippet) — [jazzguitar.be: Hal Leonard Real Books accuracy](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/songs/98423-hal-leonard-real-books-accuracy.html)
- Hal Leonard published the 6th edition in 2004 as the first legal Real Book. It dropped 137 tunes that were in the 5th (bootleg) edition and added 90 new ones. Some charts correct 5th-edition mistakes, while others follow different recordings, so the two editions don't always agree. A "European Edition" has slightly different contents. — [Wikipedia: Real Book](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Real_Book)
- Dan Reitz says the 6th edition fixes melodic problems from the bootleg editions, for example a rhythmic response figure the old book left out. He also says some fixes (e.g. "Blue Train") make those charts incompatible with the old book. — [Dan Reitz blog](https://blog.danreitz.com/?p=1128) (from the search snippet)
- There is an independent 6th-edition errata list. — [James DeCaro: Real Book (sixth edition) Errata](https://james-decaro.squarespace.com/blog/2015/5/4/real-book-sixth-edition-errata) (found but not fetched)
- An editor of the 6th edition, quoted on Hal Leonard's own page, says the book's notorious errors were fixed and the song list is nearly the same. This conflicts with Wikipedia's 137 dropped / 90 added. — [Hal Leonard Real Book Vol. I 6th ed.](https://www.halleonard.com/product/240221/the-real-book-volume-i-sixth-edition) (from the search snippet); contradicted by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Real_Book)
- Searches found no legitimate publisher listing or review for "557 Jazz Standards." The only hits were a jazz-guitar forum thread about a PDF copy ("432 pages instead of 442") and spam-style "full 557 jazz standards in Bb" Google Sites / Weebly pages. — [jazzguitar.be 557 thread](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/comping-chords-chord-progressions/2176-557-jazz-standards-pdf-3.html); [spam-style "557 in Bb" page, do not use](https://sites.google.com/view/full-557-jazz-standards-in-bb)
- Legitimate Bb alternatives that exist: Sher *The Standards Real Book* Bb edition ([Sheet Music Plus look-inside](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/look_inside?R=4900575); [Boosey listing](https://www.boosey.com/shop/prod/Various-Standards-Real-Book-Bb-Book/916110)), Sher *New Real Book Vol. 1* Bb ([SMP](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/title/the-new-real-book-volume-1-bb-edition-sheet-music/20502226?id=76665)), Hal Leonard *Real Book Vol. II 2nd ed.* Bb ([Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/240227/the-real-book-volume-ii-second-edition)), and Hal Leonard *Ultimate Jazz Fake Book* Bb edition, 625+ songs with lyrics ([SMP](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/title/the-ultimate-jazz-fake-book-bb-edition-sheet-music/2889039)).

### Inferences
- The user believes a Bb edition of the 557 is coming. I found nothing to support or refute that, so treat it as unconfirmed. Until one exists, the practical routes are (a) transposing from the C 557 (as the user already does for single tunes), or (b) checking the tune in the Hal Leonard 6th-ed. Bb Real Book or the Sher Standards Real Book Bb. Those Bb books may differ from the 557 in key, form, or melody details, so the band's 557 chart should still decide how the tune goes on the night.
- For checking melody accuracy, the user's Sher Standards Real Book (C) is likely the best of what they own, given Sher's reputation. The Hal Leonard 6th ed. is the best "what other players will expect" reference.
- The user's old bootleg Real Book Vol. 2 is the least trustworthy book for melodies. Use it last.

### Gaps
- I found no source on the 557's melodic accuracy, editors, or publisher. Its reputation is unknown.
- The Colorado Cookbook was not researched (no search was run on it).
- iReal Pro is chords-only, with no melody. That is well known but was not cited in this session.
- No systematic accuracy comparison between fake books exists in what I found. Everything is opinion.

## Q2. Melody-focused play-along books for sax: which have a recorded melody (demo) track, and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
The Hal Leonard **Jazz Play-Along** series is the clearest fit. Each tune has a split track, with the melody cue in one stereo channel and the rhythm section in the other, plus a full accompaniment track without melody. The books include lead sheets for C, Bb, Eb and bass-clef instruments. Aebersold recordings are rhythm section only: the melody is printed in the book but not played on the track. Hal Leonard's tenor-specific **Instrumental Play-Along** books include play-along tracks, and at least some include "demo" tracks. These are short collections (12 to 14 tunes).

### Cited Findings
- Hal Leonard Jazz Play-Along series: "FOR STUDY, each tune includes a split track with: melody cue with proper style and inflection" plus solo choruses and removable bass/piano. "FOR PERFORMANCE," there is "an additional full stereo accompaniment track (no melody)." The series is for "all B-flat, E-flat, Bass Clef and C instruments." The PLAYBACK+ player can slow down without changing pitch, loop, change key, and pan left/right. — [Hal Leonard Jazz Play-Along series page](https://www.halleonard.com/jazzplayalong)
- A retailer notes the melody is in the right channel and the backing in the left on the CD, so panning isolates either one. — [Musician's Friend, Duke Ellington Jazz Play-Along Vol. 1](https://www.musiciansfriend.com/books-sheet-music-media/hal-leonard-duke-ellington--jazz-play-along-volume-1-book-cd) (from the search snippet)
- Jazz Play-Along prices on halleonard.com range from about $14.99 to $32.99 (US). Examples: Vol. 2 Miles Davis $24.99, Vol. 8 Jobim $22.99, Vol. 49 Miles Davis Standards $24.99, Vol. 45 Gershwin $32.99. There are about 105 volumes listed, some online audio and some CD. — [Hal Leonard Jazz Play-Along series page](https://www.halleonard.com/jazzplayalong)
- Aebersold: the recording "provides a professional jazz rhythm section, and YOU provide the melody!" The book contains the changes and "often the melody." — [Jazzbooks.com archived page](https://web-wp.archive.org/web/20170125051607/http://www.jazzbooks.com:80/jazz/abpl) (from the search snippet)
- *Jazz & Blues: Play-Along Solos for Tenor Sax* (Hal Leonard 841442): 14 songs (Bernie's Tune, Cry Me a River, Desafinado, Fever, Fly Me to the Moon, God Bless' the Child, Harlem Nocturne, Moonglow, A Night in Tunisia, One Note Samba, Opus One, Satin Doll, Take the "A" Train, Yardbird Suite). The page describes it as "complete with play-along accompaniment tracks," with PLAYBACK+. Price $16.99. The page does not mention a sax demo track. — [Hal Leonard 841442](https://www.halleonard.com/product/841442)
- *Jazz Classics: Instrumental Play-Along, Tenor Sax*: 12 tunes (Doxy, In Walked Bud, In Your Own Sweet Way, Mercy Mercy Mercy, Nardis, On Green Dolphin Street, Reunion Blues, St. Thomas, Scrapple from the Apple, Sidewinder, Stolen Moments, Take Five). The retailer offers both a "DOXY DEMO" and a "DOXY PLAY-ALONG" sample. Price CA$18.99. — [Long & McQuade](https://www.long-mcquade.com/238826/Print-Music/Woodwind/Hal-Leonard/Jazz-Classics-Instrumental-Play-Along-Tenor-Sax-Book-Audio-Online.htm)
- *Jazz Blues Favorites* Instrumental Play-Along (tenor): the retailer page lists demo and backing audio samples. — [Long & McQuade](https://www.long-mcquade.com/238726/Print-Music/Woodwind/Hal-Leonard/Jazz-Blues-Favorites-Instrumental-Play-Along-Tenor-Sax-Book-Audio-Online.htm) (from the search snippet)
- *101 Jazz Songs for Tenor Sax* (HL 146367): a 104-page folio of melody lines with chord symbols, no audio. — [Sheet Music Plus](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/en/product/101-jazz-songs-for-tenor-sax-20164916.html) (from the search snippet)
- There is no "First 50 Jazz Standards You Should Play on Sax." The jazz-standards titles in the First 50 series are for piano and guitar. The sax title is *First 50 Songs You Should Play on the Sax* (HL 248845), a mixed-genre, 64-page solo book (Stand By Me, Summertime, Tequila...). — [Hal Leonard 248845](https://www.halleonard.com/product/248845/first-50-songs-you-should-play-on-the-sax); [First 50 Jazz Standards piano](https://www.halleonard.com/product/196269/first-50-jazz-standards-you-should-play-on-piano)
- A buyer warns that some tenor-sax "jazz standards" folios do not use the standard keys: "If you want to play these songs with others, you should find a different book." — [Musicnotes/retailer search snippet](https://www.musicnotes.com/sheet-music/scoring/instr-solo/tenor-saxophone/style/standards) (the source page for this review is uncertain)

### Inferences
- Jazz Play-Along volumes are the best match for the "listen, then learn the melody" goal. You can solo the melody channel to learn it by ear, then switch to the no-melody track to play the head yourself. The included Bb lead sheet makes them a second, independent chart for checking the 557.
- Instrumental Play-Along tenor books are arranged as solo-instrument pieces and may not be in the standard jam keys. Check each key against the 557 before relying on one for band prep.
- Aebersold is useful for the backing tracks, but it won't teach a melody by ear.

### Gaps
- I couldn't confirm for every Jazz Play-Along volume that the melody cue is played on sax. Instruments vary by volume (trumpet, sax, etc.).
- I couldn't confirm whether the *Jazz & Blues* tenor book has full-performance demos.
- Music Minus One, "Easy Jazz Favorites," and the Hal Leonard "Real Book Play-Along" were not researched (I ran out of tool budget).
- I didn't verify whether Hal Leonard's Bb Instrumental Play-Along keys match the standard keys.

## Q3. Websites with legal or free melody lead sheets and melody resources

### Takeaway
Legal lead sheets that go back to the composer exist (jazzleadsheets.com / Second Floor Music, mostly hard-bop composers). Musicnotes sells tenor-sax solo arrangements tied to specific recordings, listed by key. MuseScore community uploads vary in quality, with no audit, and have known notation and playback-pitch problems. Use them only to cross-check.

### Cited Findings
- jazzleadsheets.com: 1,258 songs from 229 composers. Melodies and changes come "straight from the composers' manuscripts or our expert transcribers." Publishing comes from Second Floor Music, Twenty-Eighth Street Music, and Minor Second Music (Don Sickler). Every song has an audio excerpt. The page doesn't say whether Bb versions are offered. — [jazzleadsheets.com/about](https://jazzleadsheets.com/about)
- Musicnotes has a tenor-sax "jazz standards" category (Autumn Leaves, Body and Soul, Donna Lee...). Listings are often tied to an artist recording and show key and range in the title (e.g. "Tenor Saxophone: G4-A5"). — [Musicnotes tenor sax standards](https://www.musicnotes.com/sheet-music/scoring/instr-solo/tenor-saxophone/style/standards) (from the search snippet)
- Virtual Sheet Music has a jazz tenor-sax catalog with a lead-sheet format filter. — [virtualsheetmusic.com](https://virtualsheetmusic.com/tenorsax/jazz/?cp=75) (from the search snippet)
- MuseScore user scores: forum threads report lead sheets that "play back perfectly" but are notated wrongly, and transposition mismatches where lead sheets "all played a whole step up." No systematic accuracy audit exists. — [musescore.org node 322258](https://musescore.org/en/node/322258); [musescore.org node 289122](https://musescore.org/en/node/289122)
- jazzstandards.com has background essays on standards (e.g. "The Evolution of Jazz Standards"). — [jazzstandards.com](https://www.jazzstandards.com/The%20Evolution%20of%20Jazz%20Standards.htm) (found but not fetched, so the per-tune recommended-recordings feature is not verified here)

### Inferences
- For a band standard, the cleanest legal melody source after the 557 is the user's own Bb fake books plus, where the tune is a hard-bop original, jazzleadsheets.com.

### Gaps
- Not researched: learnjazzstandards.com's lead-sheet offerings, Sheet Music Direct's transposition feature, Hal Leonard / Sher digital fake-book apps, Open Studio, and "Jazz Standards Bible" (Sher). I couldn't confirm whether Musicnotes offers in-app transposition.

## Q4. Original sheet music vs recordings: what teachers say about reading charts vs learning by ear

### Takeaway
Teachers agree on a combination: read the chart to get notes and rhythm right, then learn the phrasing from several recordings, ideally including a singer. A fake-book chart usually reflects one recording's version.

### Cited Findings
- John Cipolla (WKU) on learning tunes: "Practice the melody exactly as it is written. Read the melody at a slow tempo," with the metronome on 2 and 4. Keep the music in front of you "until it begins to become second nature," with the eventual goal "to play the tune by ear." "Do not embellish it. Just play it as it is written." "If there are words to the song, these should be learned as well… sing the words of the song to get the proper feel of how to phrase the melody." He credits Hal Galper / Dizzy Gillespie with "scream the melody," meaning play it confidently and correctly. — [Cipolla, "Learning Tunes" (PDF)](https://people.wku.edu/john.cipolla/Teaching_files/Learning%20Tunes.pdf)
- BetterSax: to "sound more like the musicians you listen to on the records, learn the melodies by playing along with recordings." Steps: pick a slow or medium recording, loop sections, slow to 75% (50% for fast passages), and play along "over and over again… until you can match what you're hearing." Reading a lead sheet is not wrong ("there's nothing wrong with that"), but it leads to a generic sound. — [BetterSax: How to Learn Tunes (Without the Real Book)](https://bettersax.com/how-to-learn-tunes-without-the-real-book/)
- Learn Jazz Standards: start by checking out many recordings. Frank Sinatra is "the perfect vocalist for getting an accurate melody." Bebop tunes often have no lyrics, so instrumental versions are needed. — [Learn Jazz Standards: How to learn the melody by ear](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/?p=23884) (from the search snippet)
- Jazz Advice: a Real Book chart reflects one version taken from one recording, possibly with substitute changes, a different key, or an embellished melody. — [Jazz Advice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/3-tricks-to-learning-tunes-from-recordings-that-have-nothing-to-do-with-practicing/) (from the search snippet; which Jazz Advice article said this is uncertain)
- The Talkbass wiki calls Real Book transcriptions "somewhat dubious transcriptions of specific recordings." — [Talkbass wiki](https://www.talkbass.com/wiki/jazz-really-learning-a-tune/) (from the search snippet)
- The counterpoint: the Real Book standardizes the form and roadmap of tunes that were recorded in different ways, which helps a group play together. — [jazzguitar.be Open Studio/Real Book thread](https://jazzguitar.be/forum/getting-started/85111-open-studio-real-book.html) (from the search snippet)

### Inferences
- This fits the user's goal of reading from the 557 at band without memorizing. The 557 chart is the band's shared roadmap and decides form and key. Recordings, especially vocal ones, are for phrasing. When the chart and the recordings differ on a melody note, play what the chart says at band.

### Gaps
- I found no direct teacher commentary comparing original piano/vocal sheet music with fake-book melodies.

## Q5. What a Bb tenor player should watch for (transposition, range, octave choices)

### Takeaway
Tenor sounds a major 9th below written. From a concert (C) chart, read up a whole step and add two sharps (or remove two flats), then decide the octave. Played literally, the concert line is an octave low on tenor. Charts in Bb fake books are already transposed, but the editor's octave choice still matters.

### Cited Findings
- Tenor saxophone sounds a major 9th below written. Many players read a concert chart up a whole step, but "the correct interval of transposition is a Major 9th or an octave and a whole step." Key signature: add two sharps or remove two flats. — [Best Saxophone Website Ever: transposition](https://www.bestsaxophonewebsiteever.com/?p=4799) (from the search snippet); [saxstation tenor transposition](https://saxstation.com/tenor-sax-transposition-how-to.htm)
- One search summary gave the practical tenor range as Bb3 to C6 (written). That is narrower than the instrument's standard written range of low Bb to high F/F# (palm keys), so treat that source with caution. — [intmus transposition and range](https://intmus.github.io/inttheory/12-reading-scores/a1-insttransandrange.html) (from the search snippet; possibly misreported)
- Musicnotes lists the written range of each tenor arrangement (e.g. "G4-A5"), which helps when picking a playable version. — [Musicnotes](https://www.musicnotes.com/sheet-music/scoring/instr-solo/tenor-saxophone/style/standards) (from the search snippet)

### Inferences
- Concert charts like the 557 are written for C instruments in a "singable" treble range. Moved up a major 9th, some melodies climb into the palm keys (above written high D), and for an advanced beginner it is often reasonable to drop sections down an octave. Pick one octave for the whole phrase rather than jumping mid-phrase. This is general practice and is not cited here.
- Hal Leonard Bb Real Book charts are already transposed. Their octave placement is set for trumpet/clarinet/tenor as a group, so check the range per tune.

### Gaps
- I found no cited source specifically on octave-displacement advice for tenor reading from concert fake books.
