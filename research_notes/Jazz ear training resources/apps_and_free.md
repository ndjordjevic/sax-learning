# Jazz ear-training apps, websites and free resources (as of Nov 2026)

Scope: tools for hearing jazz chord qualities (maj7, m7, 7, m7b5, dim7), chord function, progressions (ii-V-I, minor ii-V-i, turnarounds, standards) and bass movement. The reader is an advanced-beginner tenor player on a Linux desktop who probably also has a phone. Paid structured video courses are excluded.

## Q1. Which apps and sites support jazz 7th chords and ii-V progressions (platform, price, free tier, reputation)?

### Takeaway
For this reader, **teoria.com** and the **iwasdoingallright.com Online Ear Trainer** are the two most useful browser tools, and both run on Linux. Both have explicit Jazz Progressions / ii-V-I drills, and both cover all five 7th-chord qualities. The Online Ear Trainer also lets you write custom progressions. **EarMaster** has the deepest packaged jazz curriculum, but it runs only on Windows, Mac, iOS and Android, so it needs a phone or tablet here. Most general apps (Functional Ear Trainer, ToneGym, Tenuto, Perfect Ear) are fine for chord quality but weak or unverified for jazz progressions.

### Cited Findings

**Browser tools that work on Linux**

  - Chord Tones exercises use maj7, m7, 7, m7b5 and dim7. Its "Chords To Play" setting includes all five.
  - The Progressions tab has a jazz mode. Major progressions include ii-V7, V7-I, iii-vi-ii-V7-I, ii-V-I variants, turnarounds, Major Blues and the Rhythm Changes bridge. Minor progressions include ii7b5-V7b9, V7b9-i, Minor Blues and others.
  - A Custom tab lets you write your own chords and progressions in ABC-like notation.
  - It also has "Sing Over Jazz Progression" and "Play by Ear in 12 Keys" exercises.
  - Works best in Chrome, Opera or Firefox. Safari works but with lower audio quality.
  - The same author has a free iPhone app ("intervals, chords, melodies, and jazz licks").

- **teoria.com** (web)

### Inferences
- Only the browser tools (teoria, the iwasdoingallright Online Ear Trainer, skozan II-V-I, musictheory.net, ToneGym, Fifths) are confirmed to run on Linux. EarMaster's jazz workshops require the phone.
- A sensible order for this reader:
  1. 7th-chord quality ID (teoria Seventh Chord Ear Training, or the free EarMaster mobile Chord Identification with only the five 7th qualities enabled).
  2. ii-V-I and minor ii-V-i recognition (teoria Jazz Progressions, or the Online Ear Trainer's jazz mode).
  3. Custom progressions taken from the tunes being learned (the Online Ear Trainer's Custom tab).
- Functional Ear Trainer is a free, low-effort daily habit for hearing scale degrees, which supports hearing root and bass movement.

## Q2. What free resources (articles, YouTube, podcasts, courses) teach hearing changes?

### Takeaway
Free written guides from Jazzadvice and Learn Jazz Standards all teach the same method: learn the chord qualities, then follow the bass and root movement, then spot the ii-Vs. Danny Wolf's Jazz Ear Training podcast and the free-to-audit Berklee "Developing Your Musicianship" course on Coursera are the main free structured options. The Berklee course is general and fairly basic rather than jazz-specific.

### Cited Findings
- **Jazzadvice "How to Hear Chord Changes"** (free to read; the site also sells PRO content). Its method:
  1. don't trust the real book
  2. compare several recordings, starting with a simple, clear one
  3. follow the root movement and sing the roots
  4. identify each chord's quality
  5. listen for ii-Vs before resolutions, key changes and turnarounds
  6. check the solos when stuck
  - [Jazzadvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/how-to-hear-chord-changes/)
- **Jazzadvice "Fundamental Ear Training: Seventh Chords"**: play the 7th chords, identify each one, and sing the bass. [Jazzadvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/fundamental-ear-training-seventh-chords/)
- **Learn Jazz Standards** free articles:
  - "Ear Training Chord Progressions: 4 Steps" goes from intervals and chord qualities to progressions. Hearing a ii-V-I means knowing how minor, dominant 7 and major chords sound. [LJS](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/learning-jazz/ear-training/ear-training-chord-progressions/)
  - "12 Essential Jazz Chord Progressions". [LJS](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/jazz-chord-progressions/)
  - "Chord Qualities". [LJS](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/chord-qualities/)
- **Jazz Ear Training podcast (Danny Wolf)** on Apple Podcasts. For example, Episode 8 covers telling ii-V pairs apart by the interval between them. [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/jazz-ear-training/id334570315)
- **Berklee "Developing Your Musicianship"** (Coursera), part of a Musicianship specialization that includes a "Tensions, Harmonic Function, and Modal Interchange" course.
  - Can be audited free. Auditing excludes graded items and the certificate.
  - Week 1 covers the major scale, singing M2 and M3, and finding the tonal centre.
  - Reviewers call it basic for intermediate players.
  - Sources: [Class Central](https://classcentral.com/course/musicianship-1726), [Coursera specialization](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/musicianship-specialization), [course.careers review](https://course.careers/articles/is-developing-your-musicianship-worth-it)
- Practice tips from general articles:
  - Sing the guide tones (3rd and 7th) through ii-V-I. For example, B (the 3rd of G7) resolves up to C. [Jazzify](https://en.jazzify.jp/blog/jazz-piano-ear-training/)
  - Play and sing ii-V-I in several keys daily. [Serenade Magazine](https://serenademagazine.com/developing-a-jazz-ear-exercises-for-listening-and-playing/)

### Inferences
- The free articles give the method and the browser drills give the repetition. Together they cover most of what a paid course would at this level.

### Gaps
- No source was fetched this session for specific YouTube videos or playlists from Open Studio, Jens Larsen, Charles Cornell, 8-bit Music Theory, Walk That Bass, or the Learn Jazz Standards YouTube channel or podcast. These channels exist and cover ii-V and harmony topics, but specific "hear the changes" videos weren't verified. Search on YouTube for "hear ii V I by ear".

## Q3. Best 2–3 picks for this reader

### Takeaway
1. **teoria.com**: Seventh Chord Ear Training, then Jazz Progressions (II-V-I category). Free in the browser on Linux.
2. **iwasdoingallright Online Ear Trainer**: jazz progression mode (major and minor ii-V, turnarounds, blues, the Rhythm Changes bridge) plus custom progressions. Free in the browser on Linux.
3. **On the phone**: the free **EarMaster** app (free Chord Identification custom exercise; the **Jazz Workshops bundle is $14.99** if you want more), or **Functional Ear Trainer** (free) as a daily habit for scale degrees and function.

Pair these with Jazzadvice's "How to Hear Chord Changes" method on the tunes already being played.

### Cited Findings
- See the Q1 and Q2 citations: [teoria revision history](https://www.teoria.com/en/help/revision-history.php), [Online Ear Trainer](https://www.iwasdoingallright.com/tools/ear_training/online/), [EarMaster App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/earmaster-music-theory/id1105030163), [Fifths on Functional Ear Trainer](https://fifths.io/blog/best-ear-training-apps/), [Jazzadvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/how-to-hear-chord-changes/).

### Inferences
- Both browser picks are free, run on Linux and explicitly cover ii-V-I, which none of the general phone apps confirmed. That is why they rank first.
- EarMaster's jazz bundle is the best-documented paid upgrade, and it is a cheap one-time purchase on iOS.
- Chordify and iReal Pro are better used as checking and play-along tools than as ear trainers.

### Gaps
- Whether teoria's Jazz Progressions exercise is fully free wasn't verified. EarMaster's Android jazz-bundle pricing wasn't verified, since only the iOS listing was fetched.
