# Jazz ear-training books with audio (chord qualities, functions, progressions)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Reader: an advanced-beginner tenor (Bb) player who can follow changes on a play-along while reading the chart, but can't yet hear the changes. Prices are retailer list prices seen in Nov 2026 search results and may vary.

## Q1. Which books do jazz educators commonly recommend for hearing chord changes? (verified candidate list with details)

### Takeaway
Three books with audio are verified as good fits for hearing changes. **Roberta Radley, *The "Real Easy" Ear Training Book*** (Sher Music; written by a Berklee ear-training co-chair) is beginner/intermediate and built entirely around hearing chord changes. **Jamey Aebersold, *Jazz Ear Training*** is cheap and short and runs from intervals to qualities to ii-V-I. **Armen Donelian, *Training the Ear* Vol. 1/2** (Advance Music) is the deep option. Several candidates from the brief turned out wrong or have no audio. Coker's *Hearin' the Changes* has no audio I could confirm. I found no David Baker book titled "Ear Training for Jazz Musicians". Aebersold Vol. 79 is *Avalon*, not ear training.

**What educators say about how to learn this skill (no specific book named)**
- JazzAdvice: listen to the bass and root movement first, because horn players tend to hear upper structures. Identify each chord's quality (maj/min/dom/half-dim/dim). Listen for ii-Vs and turnarounds before resolutions and key changes. Compare several recordings of a standard rather than trusting a Real Book chart. — [JazzAdvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/lessons/how-to-hear-chord-changes/)
- Learn Jazz Standards' four steps: (1) know the theory (diatonic 7ths, Roman numerals); (2) learn intervals, both ascending and descending; (3) learn chord qualities; (4) find the roots via bass intervals, then the qualities, then the key center. The site points to its own quizzes, not books. — [LearnJazzStandards](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/learning-jazz/ear-training/ear-training-chord-progressions/)

### Inferences
- Aebersold *Jazz Ear Training* has Bb parts, but none of these are sax-specific. All are instrument-neutral, and most rely on singing and listening.
- Coker's *Hearin' the Changes* is a strong companion text (a catalog of progression types across ~500 tunes). Without audio, though, it works best paired with recordings or play-alongs the reader already owns, such as the Jazz Standards Playbook.

## Q2. Which suit a beginner who wants to hear ii-V-Is and standards' changes, and which are for advanced players?

### Takeaway
For this reader: **Aebersold *Jazz Ear Training*** is a cheap first pass (intervals, qualities, ii-V-I, walking bass). **Radley *Real Easy*** is the main method for hearing changes in real songs. **Aebersold Vol. 3** or HL Vol. 177 helps internalize the sound of ii-V-I while playing. Masakowski and Donelian Vol. 2 are intermediate to advanced. Coker is intermediate reference material.

### Cited findings
- Radley is labeled beginning/intermediate and starts from bass root movement and diatonic triads. — [jazzbooks.com REET](https://jazzbooks.com/jazz/product/REET)
- Aebersold JET is labeled "beginning to advanced" and starts simply with intervals. — [jazzbooks.com JETCD](https://www.jazzbooks.com/jazz/product/JETCD)
- Masakowski is intermediate to advanced and assumes basic jazz theory. — [Mel Bay](https://www.melbay.com/Products/20420M/jazz-ear-training.aspx); [Stretta](https://www.stretta-music.fi/en/masakowski-jazz-ear-training-nr-313926.html)
- Donelian Vol. 2 is intermediate to advanced. Vol. 1 is a "basic" text but long (160 pp) and expensive ($60). — [Performers Music](https://www.performersmusicchicago.com/training-the-ear-volume-2-alf-01-adv16302); [Alfred](https://www.alfred.com/products/training-the-ear-volume-1-01-adv16300)
- Aebersold Vol. 3 is rated intermediate. — [Hickey's](https://hickeys.com/music/guitar/jazz_materials/jamey_aebersold_playalongs/products/sku035003--v3-iiv7i-progressionbookaudio-set.php)

### Inferences
- A simple path for an advanced beginner: work through Aebersold JET for qualities and the ii-V-I sound, then Radley for real songs, while listening to the band's standards by focusing on the bass (JazzAdvice advice). Masakowski or Donelian come later.

### Gaps
- None of the sources lay out a beginner-to-standards sequence across these specific books. The path above is my own synthesis.
