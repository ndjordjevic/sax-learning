# Paid courses & subscriptions for jazz ear training (chord qualities, functions, progressions)

Research date: 2026-11-14. Prices are USD as shown on provider pages at fetch time; several pages are undated, so confirm on checkout. About 17 tool calls were used. Several providers in the brief could not be verified (see Gaps).

## Q1. What ear-training courses exist at the named providers?

### Takeaway
Only four verified products target hearing chords and progressions by ear: Jazzadvice's **Ear Training Method** (Vol. 4 covers jazz progressions), Learn Jazz Standards' **How to Play What You Hear**, Improvise for Real's **Recognizing Chords by Ear**, and Berklee Online's **Harmonic Ear Training: Recognizing Chord Progressions**. Open Studio has a beginner **5-Minute Ear Training** course that covers chord qualities and cadences. BetterSax has **no** standalone chord ear-training course.

## Q2. Which specifically train hearing ii–V–Is and standards' changes (not just intervals)?

### Takeaway
Explicit progression or standards training: **Jazzadvice Vol 4** (hearing any standard's progression), **Berklee OEART-320** (ii–V Lesson 10, secondary dominants), **Neff "Hearing the Changes"** (bass notes → a tune's changes), and **IFR Recognizing Chords by Ear** (functional, key-relative chords in real songs, not jazz-specific). Open Studio stops at chord qualities and cadences, and LJS's coverage is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- Jazzadvice Vol 4 "Jazz Progressions" teaches a framework for hearing any standard's progression. Vol 2 covers chord qualities and bass notes. — [Jazzadvice](https://www.jazzadvice.com/ear-training-method/)
- Berklee Lesson 10 covers "ii–V pattern and connecting ii–V patterns", along with the m7b5 and secondary dominants (both needed for minor ii–V–i). The page never uses the word "jazz". — [Berklee](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/harmonic-ear-training-recognizing-chord-progressions)
- Open Studio lists cadences with triads and 7ths, with no ii–V named. — [Open Studio](https://openstudiojazz.com/?p=522450)
- IFR teaches the "feeling of each chord in relation to the overall key" and recognizing progressions in real songs. A testimonial mentions hearing progressions in jazz. — [IFR](https://www.improviseforreal.com/?p=36052)
- Neff's "Hearing the Changes" series is about figuring out a song's changes by ear, starting from bass notes. — [Neff](https://www.neffmusic.com/blog/product/hearing-the-changes-lesson-2-bass-notes/)

### Inferences
- No verified course found names the minor ii–V–i or turnarounds explicitly in its public syllabus. Jazzadvice Vol 4 and Berklee are the most likely to cover them.

### Gaps
- No public lesson-level syllabus exists for Jazzadvice Vol 4 or LJS, so explicit minor ii–V–i or turnaround coverage is unconfirmed.

## Q4. Best value for an advanced-beginner tenor player who already owns BetterSax Foundations and the LJS Playbook

### Takeaway
1. **First, check the existing LJS relationship.** If the user's LJS access or an Inner Circle trial includes How to Play What You Hear, it costs nothing extra. Otherwise the course is $245.
2. **Best paid value overall: Jazzadvice PRO at $108/yr.** It is cheaper than the $229 standalone Ear Training Method, adds four other courses, and goes all the way to hearing standards' changes. The downsides are no refunds and a stated "not for total beginners" prerequisite.
3. **Gentler on-ramp: IFR Recognizing Chords by Ear** (functional, any instrument, 30-day refund), or **Open Studio 5-Minute Ear Training** within its 14-day trial (chord qualities and cadences only).
4. **Cheapest targeted option: Steve Neff's $9.99 lessons** (a sax teacher; Hearing the Changes, m7/maj7 hearing).
5. **Berklee OEART-320 ($1,575 credit)** is thorough and covers ii–V explicitly, but it is overkill on price and pace for this level.

### Cited Findings
- Prices and policies as cited above: [Jazzadvice PRO](https://www.jazzadvice.com/join-jazzadvice-pro-membership/), [Jazzadvice course](https://www.jazzadvice.com/ear-training-method/), [LJS store](https://cdn-m.learnjazzstandards.com/), [IFR](https://www.improviseforreal.com/?p=36052), [Open Studio](https://openstudiojazz.com/?p=1226862), [Neff](https://www.neffmusic.com/blog/product-category/jazz-lessons/ear-training), [Berklee](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/harmonic-ear-training-recognizing-chord-progressions).

### Inferences
- The Jazzadvice prerequisite ("know basic scales, chords, progressions") is roughly met by someone working through the LJS Playbook. Vols 1–2 can be done before Vol 4.
- All the singing and listening courses are instrument-agnostic, so the horn (tenor or alto) does not matter.

### Gaps
- No independent reviews were found (Reddit, Sax on the Web or Trustpilot) for the Jazzadvice Ear Training Method, LJS How to Play What You Hear or IFR chord recognition. The only reputation evidence is vendor testimonials, plus a jazzguitar.be thread (in which one poster calls Rick Beato's course "a slog"). — [jazzguitar.be](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/ear-training-transcribing-reading/94783-best-ways-ear-training.html)
