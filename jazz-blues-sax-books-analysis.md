**How to learn any standard** — goal for now: **play from the chart** (557 Bb edition or iReal Pro), know where the band is, and play correct chord notes. Memorizing is not a goal. Adapted from `reports/Efficient jazz standard learning.md` + a 2026-11-18 check of 12 YouTube teacher videos.
1. **Listen:** a short playlist (one straight version: a Sinatra/Ella vocal if it has words, the original horn version if not, plus 2–3 others) until you can hum the melody. Pick one **companion recording** (ideally a sax player) to copy phrasing from.
2. **Sing** the melody with the recording. Knowing how it sounds makes the chart easy to follow.
3. **Melody from the 557** (the band's version), played along with the recording.
4. **Name the map:** key, form (AABA, ABAC, 16-bar, blues), the ii–V–Is and turnarounds. Mark section letters on the chart if needed.
5. **Follow the chart over a backing track:** roots (root + 5th), then guide tones (3rds/7ths), then arpeggios, reading the chords and keeping your place through every section. Work the **hardest section first and most** (often the bridge).
6. **At band:** read the chart; aim to never lose your place.
7. **Improvise from the chart:** guide tones and chord tones (Playbook) → pentatonic phrases (Birch) → a phrase copied from the companion recording's solo.
8. **Done** when you can follow the chart through the whole form at band and play chord tones on every change. Then the next tune.

**Chord tones: ideas for later** (discussed 2026-11-21; start simple, add these one at a time when ready)
- **Learn chords from tunes, not all 48 up front.** Each tune teaches its chords in context; drills on the side fill in the rest.
- **Main families:** dominant 7 (1 3 5 ♭7) · minor 7 (1 ♭3 5 ♭7) · major 7 / 6 · half-diminished (1 ♭3 ♭5 ♭7) · minor 6. Later: altered dominant, diminished 7, sus4. *Autumn Leaves* on tenor has five of them: Dm7 G7 Cmaj7 Fmaj7 · Bm7♭5 E7 Am6.
- **Less to memorize than 4 × 12 × 4 = 192 notes:** the root is the chord name, the 5th is nearly always a perfect 5th, so learn the 12 major 3rds and 12 ♭7s. Other families are one-note changes from the dominant (maj7: raise the 7th; m7: lower the 3rd; m7♭5: also lower the 5th). In a dominant, 3rd and ♭7 are a tritone apart; each guide tone moves a half-step into the next chord.
- **Drone:** a root drone is enough. Pick it in concert pitch (tenor chord root − a whole step): concert B♭ drone = tenor C (fits Cmaj7, C7, Cm7); for the *Autumn Leaves* minor half, a concert G drone = tenor A. Hold each chord tone, hear its colour (3rd bright, ♭7 tense), sing it, then arpeggiate, then put a simple rhythm on one note.
- **12-key drill:** dominant 7 arpeggio 1-3-5-♭7-5-3-1 around the cycle of 4ths (C7 F7 B♭7 E♭7 A♭7 D♭7 F♯7 B7 E7 A7 D7 G7), slowly, saying each chord name. Start with C7 F7 G7 D7 A7 E7. Then: guide tones only with half-step voice-leading → other directions → minor 7 and major 7 → over an iReal one-chord vamp or cycle drill.
- **BetterSax Chord Tone Zone** drills (single chord tones in simple rhythms over a blues and *TWNBAY*) are the same idea on other tunes.
- **Next, once chord tones are decent — articulation:** Jazz Lesson Videos *(Bb) Articulation Masterclass* ([link](https://www.jazzlessonvideos.com/product-page/bb-articulation-masterclass?currency=RSD); not owned; 75+ min video + PDF, sale ≈ RSD 5,360, regular ≈ 13,940; no refunds).
