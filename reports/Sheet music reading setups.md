# Keep the iPad, add a pedal and an index

Your current setup, MobileSheets on an iPad and sometimes a TV, is already what jazz players on the forums describe as the normal way to read charts. Players who read at gigs and rehearsals mostly load fake-book PDFs into a reader app on an iPad. The only serious rival app is forScore, and it would not fix anything you are missing. The improvements that matter are small and cheap. First, build a CSV index for the 557 so every band tune opens at its page. Second, buy a reliable two-switch Bluetooth foot pedal, because a sax player's hands and mouth are both busy and MobileSheets' face gestures (mouth, smile, head turn) don't work with a horn in your mouth. Third, carry a backup device and a charger. A bigger iPad (13") or a 13.3" e-ink Boox is a nice extra, not a need, for one-page lead sheets read indoors. No app can solve the C-book problem for a tenor player: a reader cannot transpose a scanned PDF. So you have two choices: matched Bb copies laid out like the 557 page, or transposing at sight from the C chart. Some evidence is weak. Most user opinion comes from the MobileSheets forum, which leans toward MobileSheets. The main jazz forum (jazzguitar.be) could only be read through search snippets. And no survey measures how many players use tablets instead of paper.

## Tablets are now the default chart reader for players who read

Among musicians who read charts at gigs, the iPad is mainstream. One jazz-guitar forum poster who gigs regularly says it "just makes more sense" for jazz ([jazzguitar.be](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/everything-else/97309-anyone-still-using-sheet-music.html)). On Gearspace, most players who use iPads at gigs **load PDF fake books into a reader app**. They also value that an iPad on a mic stand is "always well lit", which solves the old problem of reading paper in a dark room ([Gearspace](https://gearspace.com/threads/gigging-musicians-what-are-you-using-for-charts-ipad-android-etc.865863/)). The complaints are practical. You need **a backup, "like a guitar and amp"** ([jazzguitar.be](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/guitar-amps-gizmos/61856-reliability-ipads-gig.html)). You should bring a charger, because recharging is slow ([Gearspace](https://gearspace.com/threads/gigging-musicians-what-are-you-using-for-charts-ipad-android-etc.865863/)). And some players dislike the screen glow on stage, which a dark mode (white notes on black) fixes ([TalkBass](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/tablets-for-charts-time-for-an-update.1076663/)).

The cultural objection applies to open jam sessions, not to your band. Jazz-studies etiquette guides say to memorize tunes and not read from a phone on the bandstand ([Elon University](https://www.elon.edu/u/academics/arts-and-sciences/jazz-studies/?p=1713)). Learn Jazz Standards is more relaxed and suggests bringing "an iPad with iReal Pro or a real book" in case an unfamiliar tune comes up ([LearnJazzStandards](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/learning-jazz/jazz-advice/jazz-jam-sessions-important/)). A weekly amateur band that reads from one shared fake book is exactly where a tablet fits, and that matches your goal of reading the 557 chart and keeping your place. One forum warning is worth keeping in mind: players "with their faces in books" listen less ([jazzguitar.be](https://www.jazzguitar.be/forum/recording-music-software/94406-who-uses-ireal-pro-jams-performance.html)). A good layout (one page, big type, at eye height) helps because you can glance at it instead of staring.

Bands that use tablets rarely share the PDFs themselves. Each player owns the book on their own device, and the band shares a list of titles or page numbers. MobileSheets supports this with an exported `.mss` setlist. On import it rebuilds the setlist from each member's own files, matched by title or Song ID ([MobileSheets forum](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/thread-10362.html)). Shared-library services such as BandHelper or Newzik need subscriptions for every member ([sheetmusicapps.com](https://www.sheetmusicapps.com/)). For a band that already agrees on the 557, **the page number is the shared key**, so none of that is needed.

## MobileSheets vs forScore: switching would cost money and gain little

forScore is the leader on iPad. It costs **$24.99 once, plus an optional $14.99/yr Pro subscription**, has about 44K App Store ratings at 4.8 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/forscore/id363738376)), and one independent reviewer calls it the "gold standard" ([Medium](https://medium.com/the-modern-musician/best-sheet-music-reader-for-ipad-bbbf328ced84)). MobileSheets costs **$15.99 once with no subscription**, and the purchase covers iPhone, iPad and macOS ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1620569987?l=en-us)). It is the only major reader that also runs on Android, Windows and Android e-ink tablets ([Zubersoft](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/)). Both cover what a jazz reader needs: CSV indexes to split a big songbook, setlists, Pencil annotation, half-page turns, link points for repeats, pedal support, attached audio and a metronome. MobileSheets also reads ChordPro (with chord transposition), ABC, images and .docx ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1620569987?l=en-us)). Users describe forScore as basically PDF-only ([Zubersoft forum](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/post-54560.html)).

forScore's real advantages are polish, its larger community on iPad, Apple Watch page turns through its Cue app, and **well-documented native external-display output**. It can show the music on a TV with separate display settings for the TV instead of plain mirroring ([forScore support](https://forscore.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/151000204982-displaying-your-music-on-an-external-display)). Face-gesture page turns are a Pro feature in forScore ([forScore Pro](https://forscore.co/pro/)) but included in MobileSheets. User opinion is split on half-page turns. One user calls forScore's a "disaster" that makes your eyes jump back to the top, and another says it "works flawlessly" ([Zubersoft forum](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/thread-11441.html)). MobileSheets' developer gets praise for replying to emails within a day ([TidBITS](https://talk.tidbits.com/t/choosing-a-sheet-music-manager-for-ipad/21920)). Be careful with vendor comparisons. Newzik's own "Newzik vs MobileSheets" article says MobileSheets has no iOS version and costs about $9.99, which is wrong ([Newzik](https://newzik.com/en/resources-sheet-music-app/newzik-vs-mobilesheets)).

Transposition is the point that matters most for a tenor player, and it doesn't separate the apps. **Neither MobileSheets nor forScore transposes standard notation.** MobileSheets doesn't render MusicXML at all, and users are still asking for it ([Zubersoft forum](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/showthread.php?tid=2492)). Only Newzik claims to turn a PDF into transposable notation with its OMR "LiveScore" feature, and that claim comes from the vendor and requires a subscription ([Newzik support](https://support.newzik.com/en/support/solutions/articles/77000153445-transposing-a-musicxml-part)). Your MusicXML lead sheets therefore belong in MuseScore: transpose or fix them there, then export a PDF for MobileSheets. iGigBook is the one app built around fake books. It comes with indexes for 70–90+ books and can switch between the C, Bb and Eb PDFs of the same book in the middle of a song, but you have to supply the PDFs, and users report no sync or easy backup ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id396323113); [TidBITS](https://talk.tidbits.com/t/choosing-a-sheet-music-manager-for-ipad/21920)). iReal Pro is a practice tool, not a reader. It shows chords only. Its global "Bb instrument" setting changes how chords are displayed while playback stays in concert pitch ([iReal Pro forum](https://forums.irealpro.com/threads/transpose-for-tenor-sax.9355)).

## The C-book problem: a matched Bb copy keeps you on the band's page

Horn players reading from a C book use one of four approaches. They buy the Bb edition. They transpose at sight (for tenor, read up a major 2nd and add two sharps, or remove two flats) ([Best Saxophone Website Ever](https://bestsaxophonewebsiteever.com/the-saxophonists-guide-to-the-dreaded-art-of-transposition/)). They use iReal Pro's Bb chord display. Or they re-enter the tune as notation. Opinion has been divided for a long time. In an old Sax on the Web poll, **60.7% of 28 voters preferred concert books**, mainly because a C book lets you read from the pianist's chart and costs less than buying three editions ([SOTW](https://www.saxontheweb.net/threads/transposed-real-book-or-concert.51480/page-2)). Others say a Bb book helps you learn melodies and changes faster ([SOTW](https://www.saxontheweb.net/threads/bb-fake-book-and-transposition.194039/)).

For your band, the Bb copy has one big risk: **layout drift**. If a Bb chart comes from another book, its bar lines, rehearsal letters and even its changes can differ from the 557 page. That makes "from the bridge" harder to follow, which is exactly the place-keeping skill you're working on. Your 557-derived `<Tune> - Bb Tenor Sax (from 557 Jazz Standards).pdf` files avoid this. The Bb 557 on order should too, if its page layout matches the C edition. Check one spread when it arrives. Index files bring a similar warning. One iGigBook user found a digital edition linked to the index of the print edition, which has different songs on different pages ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id396323113)). The research found **no publisher listing for a Bb 557**, and the "full 557 in Bb" download pages found online look like piracy or spam ([example, cited as a warning](https://sites.google.com/view/full-557-jazz-standards-in-bb)). Buy only from a legitimate seller. A TV projection of the C chart keeps the whole room on the same tune and form. But in a mixed C/Bb/Eb band it can't replace each player's own part, so the tenor still needs a Bb copy or has to transpose at sight.

## Hardware: a pedal is worth more than a bigger screen

**Screen size.** Musician sources call 13" the "sweet spot", the size closest to an A4 page. 11" works but "may require a little more zooming for dense sheet music" ([Newzik blog](https://en.blog.newzik.com/blog/quel-ipad-pour-la-musique)). Some advice is blunter: one violist says "only get 13" minimum" ([r/Viola](https://libreddit.winscloud.net/r/Viola/comments/1jfqadk/using_an_ipad_for_sheet_music_advice_needed)). Most of that advice comes from pianists and string players reading dense parts. A lead sheet is one staff per system with large chord symbols, so 11" with the margins cropped is usually enough. A 13" helps if you stand farther from the stand, if your eyesight is weak, or if a 557 tune runs to two pages. No source tested font size against reading distance for lead sheets, so this is a judgement, not a measurement.

**Page turns.** Sax players need a foot pedal. MobileSheets' face gestures (open mouth, smile, head turn) need a TrueDepth camera, and users report missed turns and accidental ones from head turns ([Zubersoft forum](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/thread-13499-lastpost.html)). With a mouthpiece in your mouth, mouth gestures are impossible, and head turns move the horn. In Pianobuyer's five-pedal test, the **AirTurn DUO 200 (about $99, ~150 h rechargeable)** was the most reliable and natural, and the **PageFlip Butterfly (about $90, silent, AA batteries)** was the best PageFlip. The AirTurn PEDpro was unresponsive ([Pianobuyer](https://www.pianobuyer.com/post/bluetooth-page-turning-pedals-for-pianists)). The DUO 500 now sells for about $109 ([Sweetwater](https://www.sweetwater.com/store/detail/Duo500d1)). Budget pedals such as the **Donner DBM-50 (about $48)** work with the main reader apps, though some need their key mode switched ([forScore KB](https://forscore.co/kb/donner-moukey/); [Donner](https://us.donnermusic.com/products/donner-dbm-50-bluetooth-page-turner-black)). MobileSheets' developer advises against the iRig BlueTurn because of connection problems ([Zubersoft pedals](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/pedals)). That test dates from 2022, but the models are still sold.

**Mounting.** A standing sax player reading from a seated-height stand has the screen low and far away. A **K&M 19790 tablet clamp on a mic stand (about $31–60)** puts it at eye height. It takes tablets up to about 13", but one reviewer says a thin iPad falls below its minimum clamp size, so check before buying ([Thomann](https://thomannmusic.com/km_19790_tablet_stand_holder.htm); [reviews](https://thomannmusic.com/km_19790_tablet_stand_holder_reviews.htm)).

**TV projection.** Projecting to a TV is an established technique. Newzik, forScore and Planning Center Music Stand all support external screens for showing music to a group ([Newzik](https://support.newzik.com/en/support/solutions/articles/77000467387-using-an-external-screen); [Planning Center](https://help.planningcenter.com/en/138526-connect-to-an-external-display.html)). AirPlay over Wi-Fi adds a delay that you notice on page turns. **A USB-C to HDMI cable is the dependable route** ([MacRumors](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/ipad-airplay-over-ethernet-mirroring.2293190)). Its main weakness is shape: a mirrored portrait page from a roughly 4:3 iPad sits pillarboxed in the middle of a 16:9 TV. The research found no documentation of a dedicated second-screen mode in MobileSheets for iOS, and one recent report describes a USB external-display bug on iOS 27 ([Firecore](https://community.firecore.com/t/usb-external-display-issue-on-ios-27/60782/18.md)).

**E-ink and big screens.** A 13.3" Boox running MobileSheets' e-ink build has real fans: "almost no glare", battery that lasts several gigs, and "a full-fledged replacement for paper" ([Zubersoft forum](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/thread-10777.html); [post](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/post-45041.html)). The trade-offs are slower turns that leave ghost images, no smooth auto-scroll, weaker touch, and a price of **$579–820** ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/computing/e-readers/boox-note-max-review); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/09/17/boox-tab-x-c-review-a-jack-of-all-trades-a-master-of-none)). Even in that thread, one user says e-ink's main advantage is bright sunlight. Be aware that the developer, who praises it, also sells the e-ink build. The Kindle Scribe and reMarkable can't run reader apps and don't support page-turn pedals ([droid4x](https://droid4x.com/best-e-readers-for-sheet-music-scores/); [Zubersoft forum](https://zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/post-14002.html)). The dual-screen Gvido launched at about $1,600 and is now sold out or hard to find ([Good e-Reader store](https://goodereaderstore.com/products/gvido-dual-screen-music-reader)). Two-page view on an 18" portable monitor comes out "a bit small" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/desktop-sized-displays-hit-the-road-is-it-time-for-businesses-to-trade-portability-for-productivity)). The 14.6" Galaxy Tab S11 Ultra (about $1,199–1,299) is the big-screen option on Android, but reviewers call it "big, heavy and expensive" ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-galaxy-tab-s11-ultra-review-3616532); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/tablets/samsung-galaxy-tab-s11-ultra-review)).

## Recommendations for your setup

| Decision | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Keep** | MobileSheets on the iPad | It covers every reading need, has no subscription, includes face gestures, runs on all platforms, and its developer is responsive. forScore fixes nothing you're missing. |
| **Keep** | The 557-matched Bb tenor PDFs, and MuseScore as the place to transpose or fix MusicXML | No reader app transposes PDFs, and a matched layout keeps you on the band's page. |
| **Keep** | iReal Pro and Tomplay for home practice | They are play-along tools, not readers. Set iReal Pro's global display to Bb. |
| **Add (do first, free)** | A CSV index (title, page number) for the 557 C edition, and later the Bb one, named exactly like the PDF and placed in the same folder | Every band tune becomes one tap. No public 557 index was found ([aspiers/book-indices](https://github.com/aspiers/book-indices) and the [MobileSheets forum threads](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/thread-8473.html) are where to look, then build your own). Watch the page offset and use semicolons if the import fails ([forum](https://www.zubersoft.com/mobilesheets/forum/showthread.php?tid=7933)). |
| **Add** | In MobileSheets, put the 557 C page and the Bb copy under the same song | You can flip to the C page when the leader refers to it. |
| **Add (about $50–110)** | A two-switch Bluetooth pedal: AirTurn DUO 200/500 or PageFlip Butterfly, or a Donner DBM-50 as a cheap trial | Hands and mouth are busy, and face gestures don't work while playing sax. |
| **Add (about $30–60)** | A tablet clamp on a mic stand (K&M 19790 or similar; check the thickness range for your iPad) | Puts the screen at eye height when you stand. |
| **Add** | For band nights: a charger, a phone with the same PDFs or iReal Pro as backup, dark mode or low brightness | These are the reliability habits forum players repeat most. |
| **Add (only if you use the TV)** | A USB-C to HDMI adapter instead of AirPlay | No delay on page turns. Test it after iOS updates. |
| **Consider later** | A 13" iPad, if you replace the current one | Worth it if you read standing at a distance or find two-page tunes cramped. Not worth an early upgrade. |
| **Not worth it** | Switching to forScore, Newzik/BandHelper subscriptions, e-ink readers, Gvido/PadMu, Kindle Scribe/reMarkable, portable or dual monitors, iRig BlueTurn | Each solves a problem you don't have (orchestras, outdoor sun, dense scores) or brings known drawbacks. |

## Conclusion

The research changes where the effort should go, not what you use. You already have the right software and hardware. What's missing is the **plumbing around the 557**: an index, a C/Bb pair for each band tune, and a hands-free page turn. The research also shows that transposition is a reading skill, not something software solves. Even a perfect Bb copy doesn't help when the leader points at the C page, so occasional practice transposing the C chart at sight is worth doing alongside the Bb files.

One open question remains. MobileSheets' external-display behaviour on iOS is undocumented, so test it yourself before relying on the TV for band nights. If it turns out to be mirror-only and awkward, that is the one scenario where forScore's native second-screen output would be worth its price. The pedal and the index come first either way.
